# Skala Product Principles

These are implementation guardrails. When trade-offs arise, preserve these before adding features.

## 1. Skala is not an LMS

Do not organize the product primarily around courses, modules, instructors, certificates, or catalogs. Those can exist later, but the product object is the skill and the user state is mastery over a graph.

## 2. The user should rarely choose what to do next

Skala should recommend a next best step based on prerequisites, mastery gaps, chosen domains, and recent performance. Exploration is available, but decision friction should be low.

## 3. AI is embedded, not bolted on

Avoid a generic floating assistant as the central AI metaphor. AI should appear through adaptive sequencing, Socratic challenge, contextual explanation, feedback, case roles, and evaluation.

## 4. Think before reveal

Whenever possible, require the learner to form a view before showing explanations, community perspectives, or benchmark answers.

## 5. Decision quality over answer matching

Business cases often have multiple defensible answers. Evaluate framing, evidence use, trade-offs, coherence, adaptability, and communication rather than simply matching a preferred conclusion.

## 6. Mastery must be earned repeatedly

A skill becomes credible through multiple evidence types across time. Exposure is not mastery; one excellent answer is not expertise.

## 7. Knowledge is interconnected

A skill may support multiple domains. Pricing can connect Finance, Marketing and Strategy; AI economics can connect AI and Finance. Avoid duplicated isolated course trees when one graph edge can express the relationship.

## 8. Progress should feel adult

Use streaks, levels, mastery, unlocks, challenges and percentiles carefully. Avoid cartoon rewards, confetti overload, childish badges, and meaningless XP inflation.

## 9. Expertise is an output

The profile should become a portable signal of demonstrated capability. Design evidence and scoring with future professional credibility in mind.

## 10. Domains, not rigid job roles

Structure expansion around areas of knowledge such as AI, Innovation, Applied Finance, Marketing or Growth. Do not force users into fixed Product Manager / CMO / Founder career paths.

## 11. Content quality beats content volume

A broad graph can be visible before every node is playable. A small number of excellent Skala experiences is better than hundreds of shallow AI-generated lessons.

## 12. No fake precision

Do not show Top 3%, benchmark ranks, expert labels or overly precise scores without enough evidence. Prototype data should be labeled as simulated/demo where applicable.

## 13. Dark and futuristic, not cyberpunk

The product should feel like an advanced AI research/business interface: dark-first, typographic, data-rich but sparse, subtle motion, deliberate electric accents. Avoid neon overload, glassmorphism for its own sake, robots, generic AI gradients and sci-fi decoration without function.

## 14. Every interaction should change state or understanding

Avoid dead-end content consumption. A meaningful interaction should teach, test, reveal, challenge, record evidence, or influence what happens next.

## 15. The graph is both curriculum and identity

Internally it models knowledge. Externally, “Your Skala” becomes the visible map of what the user has built and demonstrated.
