# Reference Skill V2 — Supply & Demand

Status: production authoring reference  
Program: Business Core  
Unit: 01 — Economics of Business  
Graph node: `supply-demand`  
Target time: 8–10 minutes

This document defines the first Learning Experience V2 reference skill. It supersedes the current question-heavy Supply & Demand lesson for implementation purposes.

## 1. Learning objective

After this skill, the learner should be able to:

- distinguish a **movement along demand/supply** from a **shift of the curve**;
- identify whether a market shock primarily affects demand or supply;
- predict the directional effect on equilibrium price and quantity when one curve shifts;
- explain the mechanism in business language rather than only naming the curve.

## 2. Core misconception

> “Demand increased because more units were sold after the company lowered its price.”

The learner must understand that a change in the product's own price normally changes **quantity demanded along the existing demand curve**. A demand shift requires another determinant to change.

## 3. Key ideas

1. A demand curve is a relationship between price and quantity demanded, holding other relevant conditions constant.
2. A supply curve is a relationship between price and quantity supplied, holding other relevant conditions constant.
3. A change in the product's own price normally creates a movement **along** a curve.
4. A change in another determinant can shift the entire curve.
5. Equilibrium is where intended buying and selling quantities are compatible at the same price.
6. A shift in demand or supply changes the equilibrium; the direction depends on which curve moves and where.

## Block 01 — Hook / diagnostic

**Purpose:** expose the central misconception before teaching.

### ES

**Title**  
Una empresa baja su precio 20% y vende más. ¿Aumentó la demanda?

**Choices**

A. Sí. Si vendió más, la demanda aumentó.  
B. No necesariamente. Puede haberse movido sobre la misma curva de demanda.  
C. No. Una baja de precio siempre reduce la demanda.

**Correct:** B

### EN

**Title**  
A company cuts price by 20% and sells more. Did demand increase?

**Choices**

A. Yes. Selling more means demand increased.  
B. Not necessarily. The market may have moved along the same demand curve.  
C. No. A price cut always reduces demand.

**Correct:** B

### Reveal feedback

**ES**

Si sólo cambió el precio del producto, vender más describe un aumento de **cantidad demandada**. Para decir que la **demanda** cambió necesitamos otra causa: preferencias, ingreso, cantidad de compradores, precio de un sustituto, etc.

No buscamos memorizar dos etiquetas. La distinción importa porque cambia lo que un gerente debería concluir del mercado.

**EN**

If only the product's own price changed, selling more describes an increase in **quantity demanded**. To claim **demand itself** changed, another driver must have changed: preferences, income, number of buyers, a substitute's price, and so on.

The distinction matters because it changes what a manager should infer about the market.

Evidence mode: exposure/diagnostic only.

## Block 02 — Teach 1: what a demand curve actually represents

### ES

**Heading:** Demanda no es una cifra

Una curva de demanda describe **cuánto estarían dispuestos a comprar los clientes a distintos precios**, manteniendo relativamente estables las demás condiciones relevantes.

Por eso el precio y la cantidad no son dos hechos separados. Son dos puntos de una misma relación.

Si una suscripción cuesta $15.000 y se venden 1.000 planes, ese dato no nos dice por sí solo cómo reaccionarían los clientes a $12.000 o $20.000. La curva intenta representar precisamente esa relación.

Cuando cambia **el precio del propio producto**, normalmente nos movemos a otro punto de la misma curva.

### EN

**Heading:** Demand is not a single number

A demand curve describes **how much customers would be willing to buy at different prices**, while other relevant conditions are held roughly constant.

Price and quantity are therefore not two unrelated facts. They are two coordinates in the same relationship.

If a subscription costs $15,000 and sells 1,000 plans, that fact alone does not tell us how customers would react at $12,000 or $20,000. The curve represents that relationship.

When the **product's own price** changes, we normally move to another point on the same curve.

### Key idea card

**ES:** `Precio propio cambia → movimiento sobre la curva.`  
**EN:** `Own price changes → movement along the curve.`

Evidence mode: none.

## Block 03 — Visual Explainer 1: movement along demand

### Required visual

A clean, usable chart with:

- vertical axis `Precio`;
- horizontal axis `Cantidad`;
- one clearly labeled demand curve `D1`;
- visible points `A` and `B` on the same curve;
- price guides `P1`, `P2`;
- quantity guides `Q1`, `Q2`;
- animation from A → B when price changes.

### Interaction

Control:

`Precio: alto ← slider → bajo`

The learner moves price and sees the equilibrium point move **along D1**.

The curve itself must not move.

Below the chart, show live language:

**ES**

- Precio: `$15.000 → $12.000`
- Cantidad demandada: `1.000 → 1.280`
- Curva de demanda: `D1 — sin cambio`

**EN**

- Price: `$15,000 → $12,000`
- Quantity demanded: `1,000 → 1,280`
- Demand curve: `D1 — unchanged`

### Explanatory caption

**ES:** Bajó el precio. Cambió la cantidad que los clientes desean comprar, pero no hemos introducido ninguna razón para mover toda la demanda.  
**EN:** Price fell. The quantity customers want to buy changed, but we introduced no reason for the entire demand relationship to move.

Do **not** ask the learner to drag an equilibrium dot arbitrarily between two crossed lines.

Evidence mode: none.

## Block 04 — Teach 2: what shifts demand and supply

### ES

**Heading:** Una curva se desplaza cuando cambia algo fuera de su propio eje

La demanda puede cambiar aunque el precio del producto permanezca igual.

Por ejemplo:

- entra más gente al mercado;
- aumenta el ingreso de un segmento relevante;
- mejora la preferencia por el producto;
- un sustituto se vuelve más caro;
- un complemento se vuelve más barato.

En esos casos, a **un mismo precio** los clientes ahora quieren comprar una cantidad distinta. Por eso representamos una nueva curva: `D1 → D2`.

La misma lógica aplica a la oferta. Si cambia el costo de insumos, productividad, regulación o capacidad disponible, a cada precio anterior los productores pueden querer ofrecer una cantidad diferente: `S1 → S2`.

### EN

**Heading:** A curve shifts when something outside its own axis changes

Demand can change even if the product's price stays the same.

For example:

- more buyers enter the market;
- income rises for a relevant segment;
- preferences improve;
- a substitute becomes more expensive;
- a complement becomes cheaper.

At the **same price**, customers now want a different quantity. That is a new demand curve: `D1 → D2`.

The same logic applies to supply. If input costs, productivity, regulation or available capacity change, sellers may want to offer a different quantity at every previous price: `S1 → S2`.

### Key idea card

**ES:** `Condición externa cambia → puede desplazarse toda la curva.`  
**EN:** `External condition changes → the entire curve may shift.`

Evidence mode: none.

## Block 05 — Worked example: the gym

### Scenario

A premium gym charges `$45.000` per month and has `800` active memberships.

A nearby competitor closes permanently. The gym keeps its own price unchanged for two weeks and receives materially more applications.

### Skala reasoning

**Step 1 — What changed?**  
The gym's own price did not change. A competitor disappeared.

**Step 2 — Which side of the market is affected first?**  
Customers who previously considered the competitor now have fewer substitutes. At the same gym price, more customers are willing to join.

**Step 3 — Representation**  
Demand shifts right: `D1 → D2`.

**Step 4 — Equilibrium implication**  
If capacity and supply conditions are unchanged, upward pressure appears on both equilibrium price and quantity.

**Step 5 — Managerial meaning**  
The company should not interpret the extra applications as proof that its previous price cut or campaign caused growth. Market structure changed.

### Visual

Show:

- D1 and D2;
- S1 fixed;
- old equilibrium E1;
- new equilibrium E2;
- arrows `P1 → P2` and `Q1 → Q2`.

### Prompt

**ES:** ¿Qué dato adicional buscarías antes de decidir subir el precio?  
**EN:** What additional evidence would you seek before deciding to raise price?

This prompt is reflective and does not need mastery weight.

## Block 06 — Guided Market Lab

This replaces the current low-usability point-placement interaction.

### Interaction rule

For each shock, the learner answers **before reveal**:

1. What shifts first?
   - Demand
   - Supply
   - Neither / movement along

2. Direction?
   - Left
   - Right
   - Along the curve

3. Predict equilibrium:
   - Price ↑ / ↓ / ambiguous
   - Quantity ↑ / ↓ / ambiguous

Then press `Mostrar resultado`.

The chart animates the relevant curve and explanatory feedback appears.

### Shock A — A competitor exits

Correct:

- Demand → right
- Price ↑
- Quantity ↑

**ES feedback**  
A un mismo precio, parte de los compradores que antes consideraban al rival ahora están disponibles para este producto. La demanda se desplaza a la derecha.

**EN feedback**  
At the same price, some buyers who previously considered the rival are now available to this product. Demand shifts right.

### Shock B — Input cost rises sharply

Correct:

- Supply → left
- Price ↑
- Quantity ↓

**ES feedback**  
Producir cada unidad se volvió menos atractivo a los precios anteriores. Los vendedores ofrecen menos: la oferta se desplaza a la izquierda.

**EN feedback**  
Producing each unit became less attractive at previous prices. Sellers offer less: supply shifts left.

### Shock C — A close substitute becomes cheaper

Correct:

- Demand → left
- Price ↓
- Quantity ↓

**ES feedback**  
Parte de la disposición a comprar migra hacia el sustituto. A cada precio anterior, la cantidad demandada del producto original es menor.

**EN feedback**  
Some willingness to buy moves to the substitute. At each previous price, quantity demanded for the original product is lower.

### Shock D — Productivity improves

Correct:

- Supply → right
- Price ↓
- Quantity ↑

**ES feedback**  
La empresa puede producir más al mismo costo o la misma cantidad a menor costo. La oferta se expande.

**EN feedback**  
The firm can produce more at the same cost or the same amount at lower cost. Supply expands.

### Shock E — The company lowers its own price

Correct:

- Neither curve shifts
- Movement along demand

The visual must move the point along D1 rather than shifting D1.

**ES feedback**  
Cambió el precio del propio producto. Sin otra información, eso no basta para mover la demanda completa.

**EN feedback**  
The product's own price changed. Without another change, that is not enough to shift the entire demand curve.

### Guided evidence

Store correctness and misconception family for adaptation, but keep weight below independent transfer/application.

## Block 07 — Application: two things happened at once

### ES scenario

Un gimnasio premium baja su precio de `$48.000` a `$42.000` el lunes. Ese mismo día, un competidor cercano anuncia que cerrará definitivamente el viernes. Durante las dos semanas siguientes, las nuevas inscripciones aumentan 55%.

**Prompt**

Separa los dos mecanismos que podrían explicar el aumento y explica qué evidencia buscarías para estimar cuánto corresponde a cada uno.

### EN scenario

A premium gym cuts price from `$48,000` to `$42,000` on Monday. The same day, a nearby competitor announces it will permanently close on Friday. Over the next two weeks, new sign-ups rise 55%.

**Prompt**

Separate the two mechanisms that could explain the increase and explain what evidence you would seek to estimate the contribution of each.

### Evaluation anchors

Strong response should distinguish:

- movement along D from own price cut;
- rightward shift in D from competitor exit;
- need for timing/control/comparison evidence;
- awareness that raw before/after volume cannot identify both effects cleanly.

Evidence mode: application.

## Block 08 — AI challenge

The AI challenge should be generated from the learner's application response, but the challenge objective is fixed.

Primary challenge goal:

> Force the learner to state what evidence could falsify or weaken the preferred explanation.

Examples:

- “Suppose sign-ups increased equally in neighborhoods where the competitor never operated. What happens to your competitor-exit explanation?”
- “Suppose traffic rose before the price cut was visible. Which mechanism becomes less convincing?”
- “What observation would make you conclude this was mainly a movement along demand rather than a demand shift?”

AI should not reteach the whole lesson here.

Evidence mode: application/adaptability.

## Block 09 — Independent transfer

### ES

Un impuesto nuevo aumenta el costo de producir bebidas azucaradas. La demanda de consumidores no cambia inicialmente.

¿Qué predicción es más consistente con el modelo?

A. La oferta se desplaza a la izquierda; precio de equilibrio sube y cantidad baja.  
B. La demanda se desplaza a la derecha; precio y cantidad suben.  
C. Sólo hay un movimiento sobre la demanda; la oferta no cambia.  
D. Precio y cantidad necesariamente permanecen iguales.

**Correct:** A

### EN

A new tax raises the cost of producing sugary drinks. Consumer demand is initially unchanged.

Which prediction is most consistent with the model?

A. Supply shifts left; equilibrium price rises and quantity falls.  
B. Demand shifts right; price and quantity rise.  
C. There is only movement along demand; supply does not change.  
D. Price and quantity necessarily remain unchanged.

**Correct:** A

### Feedback

Even on a correct answer, explain:

> Higher production cost changes sellers' willingness to supply at previous prices. The structural shock is therefore on supply. The new equilibrium has higher price and lower quantity, all else equal.

Evidence mode: recall/application, independent.

## Block 10 — Mastery summary

Do not make the summary primarily about a score.

### ES

**Lo que acabas de aprender**

- El precio del propio producto suele mover la cantidad sobre una curva existente.
- Preferencias, ingresos, sustitutos, costos y otras condiciones pueden desplazar una curva completa.
- Un cambio de equilibrio sólo tiene sentido si puedes explicar **qué cambió primero y por qué**.

**Ahora deberías poder:** identificar el mecanismo detrás de un shock de mercado antes de atribuir una variación de ventas a precio, marketing o competencia.

Then show evidence/mastery state separately and clearly label it as evidence-derived.

### EN

**What you just learned**

- A product's own price usually moves quantity along an existing curve.
- Preferences, income, substitutes, costs and other conditions can shift an entire curve.
- An equilibrium change is meaningful only if you can explain **what changed first and why**.

**You should now be able to:** identify the mechanism behind a market shock before attributing a sales change to price, marketing or competition.

# 5. Interaction usability requirements

The new market visual must:

- use actual SVG/canvas/accessible chart primitives or equivalent, not decorative crossed CSS lines;
- label `D1`, `D2`, `S1`, `S2`, `E1`, `E2` when relevant;

- preserve Skala's dark/editorial visual language without turning the chart into neon decoration.

## 7. Acceptance criteria

The reference skill is accepted only if a learner can finish it and accurately explain, in their own words:

1. movement along a curve vs shift of a curve;
2. one demand-shift cause;
3. one supply-shift cause;
4. why the gym scenario contains two simultaneous mechanisms;
5. why placing an equilibrium point without identifying the mechanism is insufficient.

Product acceptance additionally requires:

- no arbitrary dot-drag task;
- at least two real teaching blocks visible in the experience;
- worked example present;
- guided feedback explains mechanism;
- chart is usable on mobile;
- ES and EN content both present;
- evidence remains derived from learner actions, not reading progress.
